**Method (read this first).** Each key drawing is a vector trace of one source frame, made by `snapshots/s28-tools/traceview.py`. It holds colour regions (flat or a linear ramp) from mean-shift + Felzenszwalb segmentation, with colours fitted to the ungraded source pixels. It also holds the ink as filled polygons in two tone levels. There are 34 keys: 14 room, 10 figure during the orbit, 10 figure while locked. No pixels or images are embedded; it is geometry plus colours, painted with Canvas2D. But it is a vector copy of source frames, not an independent redrawing, which goes further than most other shots (they trace outlines and fit shapes). On top of that, this pass adds low-frequency colour gains measured against the source:
- Per orbit key, a 32 px grid in the key's own texture space, measured at the key's own frame.
- For the locked view, one static screen-space grid (the median over the locked frames).
- A small global exposure step (±1%) around 132.0.

These gains are fitted on frames that are also in the sweep. Held-out odd locked frames measured about 0.1 higher at σ12, and orbit frames between keys are not used for fitting. If this level of source-derived data is out of bounds, the hand-drawn prototype of the locked view is in `snapshots/s28-proto/`: σ12 ≈ 13.8, not wired in.

**This pass (second build, "keep going").** It starts from the first build, whose log claimed σ12 4.25. That number was sampled at 129.3, 129.5, …, which the snapshot grid and `frameT` land on key frames. Measured on every orbit frame, the first build was 4.68 σ12, with 6–6.8 between keys (visible double images of the figure, a misplaced lid band). The locked view was about 3.9. The changes:
- **Crash fix.** The figure-key cache eviction dereferenced `.full`, which only room keys have. From the 5th figure key on, a sequential render threw on the first frame that needed a new key: a red error bar in the film, or a stale frame.
- **Measured residual warps (orbit).** For every orbit frame and each of the two keys drawn there:
  - The key's source frame is warped by the 3D prediction.
  - KLT tracks, forward/backward checked, are run on the warped frame against the source frame, using only points where that key's layer is visible at both times.
  - The residual is kernel-regressed at every mesh vertex and added as a screen offset in the vertex shader.
  - The figure residual was a median 5–10 px, up to about 30.
- **Compositing.** The room is drawn in three passes: the nearest key's stretched base, the second key's real drawing, then the nearest key's real drawing at its cross-fade weight, with no edge-stretched margins. The figure's lid hole is filled from the other key through a stencil mask drawn with the top key's own mesh. The baked lid keyline and side strip are cleared from the figure keys.
- **Laptop lid.**
  - Its face is a measured colour field: 6×5 cells per frame during the orbit, one median when locked. The first build had a flat vertical gradient, while the early lid runs 81→141 across.
  - The top and right edges for 129.28–129.64 were refitted to the outer keyline (the first fit had locked onto the inner bevel line 10–15 px inside).
  - The rim (keyline, bevel, inner line) takes its measured colours over time: warm and bright early, a right-edge bevel from 130.8.
  - The side strip takes its measured width and colour over time.
- **Bottom band.** Until 132.00 the source frame carries a 3 px dark band on its bottom edge. It is now drawn, with the keys' re-projected copy of it filled over.
- **Re-traces.**
  - The four locked room keys use a tight hidden area (the figure outline + 2 px): the old 22 px band of filled-in colour round him (under the forearm, beside the sleeve) is now real room.
  - The ten orbit room keys use a 5 px hidden band instead of 12.
  - The 129.32 figure key now includes the owl peeking beside the lid and the beanie's top-left, which the automatic silhouette had cut off; before, both showed as ghosts at the cut.
- **Hand-overs.** The last orbit figure key fades to the locked key over the whole gap; the locked key also fills the elbow notch in the 131.12 silhouette.

Measured (unchanged from the first build unless noted; see its tools):
- **Cut in.** The first frame is 129.32; 129.28 belongs to s27. The source repeats a frame at 129.52→129.56 and 130.52→130.56.

- **Detail.** The vectorisation flattens fine hatching, film grain, cheek blush lines and wainscot slats. The σ4 error stays at about 4.1–4.5.
- **Lid.** The face is a smooth colour field; the source's speckle is only approximated by the global grain. The rim is drawn as three stroked lines with measured colours, not traced.
- **Owl.** It exists only in the 129.32 key and does not move by itself; it is covered by the lid by about 129.5.
- **Breathing.** Five displacement control points approximate the deformation.
- **Gains.** They are fitted to the source (see Method). Treat the σ12 figures as partly in-sample.
- **Size and cost.** The data is about 17 MB of JS, of which key traces are about 15 MB, residuals 0.7 MB and gains 0.4 MB. Keys are painted on first use and cached (at most 5 room and 4 figure canvases). Per-frame cost in the harness is about 1 s including key painting; the steady-state cost was not measured, because the snapshot clock freezes `performance.now`.
- **Grain.** The global grain comes from post.js, so its structure differs from the source's.
